# One small design system, a blue accent and a slim sidebar

The UI was restyled after a reference design the Owner liked: a CRM table with a light-grey canvas, white cards, a quiet sentence-case table, tinted pill badges, outline buttons, underline tabs, a green accent and a wide navy sidebar. We kept the look but made two deliberate departures from it.

**The accent stays blue.** In this app green already means something: added lines in a diff, an "Edited" Video and a finished Run. A green Apply button would look like a success label, right next to green diff lines on the Review screen. Blue means "an action you can take", green means "added or done", amber means "waiting" and red means "failed or destructive". These tones are the `Tone` type in `frontend/src/components/ui/tone.ts`, and the run-status labels map to them in one place, `frontend/src/lib/runLabels.ts`.

**The sidebar is slim and follows the theme.** The reference's sidebar holds about a dozen pages. This app has two (Videos and History) plus a link to How It Works, so a wide navy sidebar would sit mostly empty and would need its own dark-mode design. Instead, from 1024 px up there is a narrow sidebar on the surface colour. It holds the nav, and at the bottom the quota, the Fake-mode badge, the Channel card and the theme toggle, the way the reference shows its user card. Below 1024 px a compact top bar opens the same nav as a sheet, so nothing wraps at 390 px.

The app's look lives in two places. Colour, shadow and font tokens are in `frontend/src/index.css` (light under `@theme`, dark under `.dark`). A handful of primitives in `frontend/src/components/ui/` (Button, Badge, Card, Alert, Tabs, Segmented, SearchInput, PageHeader, BackLink, Count and the shared input classes in `inputs.ts`) cover the rest. Each takes its variations as props (`variant`, `tone`, `size`), not as extra classes: Tailwind doesn't promise that a class passed later beats the primitive's own, so a `className` is only for layout such as margins and widths. Screens compose these instead of repeating class strings, so a later restyle means editing the tokens and primitives, not every screen. We chose not to adopt a component library (shadcn, MUI): the app needs about ten small pieces, and a library would add weight and its own theming model for no gain. Icons come from `lucide-react`.

The static pages (How It Works, privacy, terms) can't use Tailwind, so `frontend/public/pages.css` holds a hand-copied set of the same tokens; change it together with `index.css`. Those pages follow the system's light or dark setting, not the app's theme toggle, since they're served without the app. Their brand header has one source, `scripts/how_it_works.py`, which writes it into all three pages.
